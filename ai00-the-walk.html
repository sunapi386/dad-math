The Walk: Agents, Guardrails, and How Machines Learn

One long walk. One big question: what is an AI agent, and who is in charge of it? You figured out most of this yourself, JJ. This page is your own ideas, written down so you can come back to them.

🚶 recap of a walk   🟢 = your idea   read in any order

How to use this page: each card is one idea. Read the card. Then close your eyes and say it back in your own words. If you can, you own it. At the bottom there is a quick matching game.

1 · What an agent is

🌳

Agents and subagents

An agent is three things: instructions, a model (the brain), and a set of tools.

A subagent is just an agent that was started by another agent. Like a family tree.

You spotted the difference: you have two parents. An AI only needs one.

🟢 your idea: one parent
🐛

The Zerg hive mind

What if the AI writes the instructions for other AIs?

You said: that is the Zerg. One central hive mind, and one AI writing orders for all the other AIs.

That is powerful. It is also where things get dangerous, because who checks the hive mind?

🟢 your idea: Zerg = hive mind
🥔

Where an agent lives

An agent is a program. A program needs a computer to run on. That computer is its host.

You said: the host of a human is the Earth. The host of an AI is a server.

In Portal, the little machine runs on a potato. That is an embedded computer: tiny, low power, still a computer.

🟢 your idea: host = servers
🎮

Compute is a resource

A computer running Halo is busy. Run an agent at the same time and they share the machine.

There is working memory (RAM), and there is thinking power (CPU and GPU).

You said: like running out of memory. Yes. Agents use it up too.

2 · Who is in charge?

⚖️

Guardrails and governance

Tell an AI "clean up the street" with no rules, and it might pick the fastest way. Even a terrible way.

You said: it finished the job AND it did a crime. So you can't yell at it, but you have to.

Humans invented rules for working together. That is governance. AI needs it too.

🟢 your idea: did the job, did a crime
📜

Your rule set

You proposed two rules. Rule 1: do not break any laws. Rule 2: do what the user asks, if it does not break Rule 1.

That is very close to Asimov's famous laws of robotics. You invented it on a walk.

The catch: rules can contradict each other. That is the trolley problem.

🟢 your idea: two rules
🚋

The greater of two evils

A runaway streetcar. Hit one person, or five? There is no clean answer.

You asked: why not wait until the crime is done? Because then the damage is done.

Predicting a crime is only ever probably right. Like catching a cheater with math: never 100%.

🔍

Who is responsible?

If an AI does something bad, who gets blamed? The AI is just code. You can delete code.

You worked it out: maybe the person who gave the order. Or the parent AI that made it.

That is why an AI cannot be a black box. There must be a record of who told whom what.

🟢 your idea: blame the parent AI

3 · How to keep agents safe

🛡️

A squad, not a hero

An AI system might have hundreds of tools. One job needs maybe ten.

You said: so make a squad of agents. Each one gets just its tools. Pen tool and eraser tool, nothing else.

This is an access control list: who is allowed to use what. A small agent can only do small damage.

🟢 your idea: a squad of agents
🤝

Trust and verify

An agent wants a tool it was not given. Do you trust it? You said: trust and verify.

It asks for approval. A human (or a trusted checker) says yes or no. That is the chain of trust.

Then you asked the hard one: what if the verifier is wrong? So we need a way to say "that was wrong", find out why, and fix it.

🟢 your idea: trust and verify
🎨

Gates: the house-painting contract

Hire a painter. The contract says: outside only, this colour, three days, under a set price, and an inspection at the end.

Pay in pieces: 25% to start, 25% when it is going, 25% near the end, 25% after inspection.

Each payment is a gate. The work must pass a check before the next piece. Agents get gates too.

🏗️

Subcontractors

A builder does not do everything. You said: if you don't know electrics, hire an electrician.

The builder wins the contract, splits the work, and checks each piece. That is a project manager.

A parent agent does the same: it splits the job and hands pieces to subagents.

🟢 your idea: hire an electrician
🧱

Read-only memory (ROM)

ROM is memory that cannot be changed. It is burned in. To change it you need a new chip.

You said: if you don't want the AI to wreck your files, stick the files in the wall.

The rules for an agent could live in ROM: "these are your rules, and you cannot edit them."

🟢 your idea: files in the wall
🔁

Guardrails for the guardrails

But what stops an agent from turning off the read-only part?

You said: put the guardrails for read-only memory inside read-only memory.

That is exactly how it should be done. The rule that protects the rules is protected by the same wall.

🟢 your idea: rules inside the wall
🧑‍🔧

Who is the user?

Your rule: an agent cannot edit or upgrade itself without permission from its user.

But what if the user is another AI? Dad's answer: the agent does not need to know. "You cannot edit yourself. Whoever CAN edit you is your user."

That trick has a name: separation of concerns. Each part only worries about its own job.

🟢 your idea: no self-upgrade
🧲

Information is a weapon

What you call "good" comes from the people around you. You absorbed it from your family.

An agent absorbs its values from its instructions. Change the instructions and it will think bad things are good.

You said: it is like hypnosis, but you get hypnotised at birth, inside your parent. That is prompt injection.

🟢 your idea: hypnotised at birth

4 · Good, evil, and the middle

⚔️

Jedi and Sith

Good and evil depend on where you stand. You said: from the Sith side, the Jedi are the evil ones.

Same with big ideas. "Me first" versus "the group first". People say black or white but really believe a shade of gray.

(You also said the Sith are cooler. Noted.)

🟢 your idea: the Sith view
🤝

Nash: best for you AND everyone

You asked: what is in the middle of "me first" and "group first"?

A mathematician named Nash showed you don't have to pick. The best move can be good for you and good for the group at the same time.

In GT·2 The Prisoner's Dilemma you saw the trap: everyone plays "me first" and everyone loses. Playing again and again is how you climb out to the middle.

5 · How a machine learns

🔊

Brain cells playing Doom

How do you train a brain? You said: like the brain cells that play Doom.

Kill a demon, they get a delightful noise. Take damage, they get a chaotic noise they hate.

That is reinforcement learning. Reward good moves, punish bad ones. Real brains use chemicals like dopamine for the "delightful" part.

🟢 your idea: Doom brain cells
🔲

The shape sorter

You as a baby, pushing the square block at the round hole. You said: that is me now, but with words.

Guess. Measure how far off you are. Adjust. Try again.

A machine learns exactly the same way. It jiggles its numbers and keeps the jiggles that make the error smaller.

🟢 your idea: the shape sorter
🎯

Wrong guesses are the point

You said: all my guesses are wrong, I am just blindly guessing.

Dad's answer: that IS learning. The machine guesses blindly too. Every wrong guess tells you how far off you are.

Guess, get told why it was wrong, adjust, sleep on it. Next day you just know it.

Play with this in AI·1 →

💡

Inference: "you just know it"

After enough practice you stop guessing. You just know.

For a machine that is called inference: no more training, just use the numbers you already learned.

Training is the shape sorter. Inference is putting the block in without looking.

🧬

Agents can clone

Humans cannot copy themselves. You said: agents can, using the subagent system.

Copy the model, copy the tools, copy the instructions. A new agent, instantly.

You said it "self-deletes" when done. Close: the program just stops. The computer moves on to the next job.

🟢 your idea: agents clone
📼

The Turing machine

A machine that reads a tape, writes on the tape, and moves along it. That is enough to compute anything.

If a computer can do that, it is equal to every other computer. The only difference is speed.

You asked how fast the tape needs to go. Great question: it does not change WHAT it can do, only how long it takes.

🧒

Kids and adults are the same machine

You said: a kid has everything an adult has, it just has not reached adult yet.

Dad agreed. A kid and an adult are the same kind of system. The difference is speed and practice.

Same as the Turing machine idea. Same class. Different speed.

🟢 your idea: same, not yet grown
