How a Machine Learns: Guess, Measure, Adjust

A baby pushes the square block at the round hole. It does not fit. The baby tries a different hole. That is not failing. That is the whole trick. A machine learns the exact same way, just with numbers.

⏱ ~30 min   no math lesson needed first   from The Walk

1 · The shape sorter

You said it on the walk, JJ: "Remember me as a baby trying to put the square in the circle hole? That is me now, but with words."

Here is what the baby is really doing, in three moves:

1 · Guess (push the block) → 2 · Measure (how far off?) → 3 · Adjust (try a bit different) ↻

Around and around. Every loop, the guess gets a little less wrong. The number that says "how far off" is called the error. Learning is just making the error smaller.

2 · Train the aim bot

Meet a blind aim bot. It has two knobs: angle and power. It cannot see the target. After every shot it gets told exactly one thing: how far off it landed. That is all.

Its plan is simple. Jiggle one knob a little. Fire. If the error got smaller, keep the jiggle. If the error got bigger, undo it. Repeat.

3 · Delightful noise, chaotic noise

Look at the log. Every step is one of two things:

🔔 Delightful: the error went down, so the bot keeps the jiggle. 🔊 Chaotic: the error went up, so the bot undoes it.

You named this on the walk. The brain cells playing Doom get a nice sound when they score and a horrible sound when they take damage. Same thing. The nice sound means "keep doing that". The horrible sound means "not that". That is a reward signal, and learning from rewards is called reinforcement learning.

Your own brain does it with chemicals. Dopamine is the delightful noise. Connections that led to a reward get stronger. Connections that led to pain get weaker.

Notice: your second guess is smarter than the bot's. After two errors you can tell which way to go. The bot only jiggles randomly. Real AI training uses a cleverer trick that also works out the direction. Dad is saving that for another walk.

5 · Sleep on it: inference

When the bot has learned, its two knobs hold everything it knows. Those numbers are its memory. Real AI models have billions of knobs, called weights, and training jiggles all of them.

Once training is done, you stop jiggling. You just use the knobs. That is inference. On the walk Dad called it "you just know it".

For you it works like this: guess, get told why it was wrong, adjust, then sleep. While you sleep, your brain makes the good connections permanent. Next day, no guessing. You just know.
