Splat Lab: Painting a World out of Fuzzy Blobs

In Topic 1 the aim bot learned with guess, measure, adjust. Dad promised that same loop could do something big. Here it is: the SAME loop paints a whole 3D scene, out of nothing but fuzzy blobs of colour. Grown-ups call this Gaussian splatting. A research paper from 2023 set it loose.

⏱ ~30 min   do Topic 1 first   from The Walk

1 · What is a splat?

A splat is a fuzzy blob of colour. Brightest in the middle, fading to nothing at the edges. No crisp edge anywhere. That is different from the triangle in the Quest, which has a hard line you could cut yourself on.

The soft fade has a name. If you walk across a splat and write down how bright it is, you get a smooth bump. Grown-ups call that bump a Gaussian. So a splat is a bump of colour, sitting on the page.

2 · Squish and spin

A blob does not have to be a circle. Stretch it into an oval and spin it. A long thin oval is a blade of grass, a hair, the edge of a table. A fat wide oval is a wall or a patch of sky. Real splats are all shapes, and most of them are squished.

3 · See-through

Every splat is a little see-through. Its see-through number is called alpha. Alpha 1 is solid paint. Alpha 0 is invisible. Stack two see-through blobs and the colours blend where they overlap, like two sheets of coloured cellophane.

4 · Who is in front? (sorting)

You just saw it: when see-through blobs overlap, the one painted last wins. So the computer has to paint the far-away blobs first and the close ones last, like laying down cellophane from the back of the pile to the front.

Before every single frame, the computer sorts every splat, back to front, by how far away it is. Skip the sort and the picture glitches.

🗂️ Back to front

Three blobs: a far sky, a middle tree, a near ball. Turn sorting off and tap paint again a few times. Watch the ball vanish behind the sky.

Painting order: sky → tree → ball. Far first, near last.
Things to try: ① Sorting off, paint again five times. How many times does it look wrong? ② Swap the ball and the tree, sorting on. Which one wins the overlap now? ③ Drag the ball so it does not overlap anything. Does sorting still matter? ④ Say the rule out loud: far first, near last.

5 · Make a picture out of blobs

Here is the big idea: a whole picture is just many splats, each in the right spot, the right size, the right colour. No lines, no shapes, just blobs. Enough blobs, placed well, and your eye stops seeing blobs and starts seeing a smiley.

6 · Guess, render, compare, adjust (training)

You placed every blob by hand. The computer does not. It starts with a random mess of blobs, renders its picture, compares it to the real photo, and nudges a blob to make the difference smaller. Keep the nudge if the error went down. Undo it if the error went up.

1 · Guess (random blobs) → 2 · Render (paint them) → 3 · Compare (error) → 4 · Adjust (nudge one) ↻

That is the aim bot from Topic 1, exactly. The aim bot had two knobs. This one has seven knobs per blob, times forty blobs. Same loop. Just more knobs.

🤖 Let the computer learn the picture

Press Train one step to watch one nudge. Press Run and watch the error shrink. It tries a nudge both ways, keeps the better one.

Notice: this toy tries one knob at a time, both ways, and keeps the better one. Real splatting is cleverer: it works out the downhill direction for all the knobs at once and steps that way. That direction is called the gradient. Dad is saving the full trick for another walk.

7 · Grow and prune

Two more tricks make the real thing work. Where the picture is still wrong, the computer splits a blob into two smaller ones, so there are more blobs to add detail. And any blob whose alpha has faded to almost nothing gets thrown away, because painting an invisible blob is wasted work.

Grow where it is wrong. Prune what you cannot see. The blob count goes up and down while the error keeps going down.

8 · The blobs float in 3D

Here is the real magic. The splats are not stuck on flat paper. They float in space, all around a real object, like a swarm of tiny coloured clouds.

You take photos from many angles. The computer runs the guess, render, compare, adjust loop against every photo at once, so the blobs end up in the spots where the picture looks right from every side. Then you can fly the camera anywhere, even to places you never took a photo.

Then guess the real number.

9 · Where you have seen it, and why it matters

Spin your phone around your Lego build, taking photos the whole way round. A few minutes later the build appears inside a game, and you can walk around it from any side. That is splats. Real captures use millions of them.

Three threads tie together here, JJ:

Learning. It is Topic 1's guess, measure, adjust loop, run against photos instead of a target on the ground.
Flattening. To paint a floating blob on the screen, divide its size by its distance. That is the Sketch Lab's glass, the same 1-over-distance you met in Flatten.
Drawing a world. The Quest asked how a machine draws a world. Triangles were one answer. Fuzzy blobs are a second answer, and this one can learn from photos.

📓 Homework (out loud, with Dad)

  1. Find the blobs. Look at a tree outside. If you had to paint it with only fuzzy ovals, what shape would the leaves be? The trunk? The sky behind it?
  2. Sort the room. Pick five things you can see from your chair. Say them in the order the computer would paint them: far first, near last.
  3. Challenge: the toy in section 6 nudges one knob at a time. Invent a way to nudge two knobs at once and still know whether to keep it.
